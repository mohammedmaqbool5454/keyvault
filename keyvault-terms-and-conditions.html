About KeyVault

What the App does — and what it deliberately does not do.

KeyVault is a password manager for Android. It lets you store logins, passcodes, and passkeys in an encrypted vault that lives on your device. Its main features include:

  • Vault — storing, searching, sorting, and organising passwords, passcodes, and passkeys, with favourites and recently used items.
  • Local encryption — your vault is encrypted on your device and is only unlocked with your master password or, where you enable it, your fingerprint.
  • Generator — creating strong, random passwords and passphrases.
  • Security checks — identifying weak and reused passwords, checking passwords against known breach data, and helping you upgrade supported logins to passkeys.
  • Passkey support — creating, storing, and using passkeys on supported Android versions, and acting as a credential provider for other apps and websites.
  • Notes and attachments — adding notes and attaching files (any file type, up to 10 MB per file) to passwords, passcodes, and passkeys. Attachments are stored encrypted inside the vault on your device, like everything else in it.
  • Website and App picker — when adding an item, pick from a built-in list of popular websites, or from the apps installed on your device, which are read locally on the device. When the website picker is opened in Online mode, the sites’ icons (favicons) are fetched from Google’s favicon service.
  • Open a saved website — where an item has a website address, open it in your browser from the item’s detail view (Online mode).
  • Backup and restore — exporting and importing encrypted backups on your device, and, if you choose to enable it, backing up to your own Google Drive.
  • Import — importing passwords from a Chrome password export (CSV) and from KeyVault backup files.

KeyVault is designed so that Zetro Labs does not operate a server that holds your vault. We have no account system for your vault data, we cannot read your stored credentials, and there is no “sign in to KeyVault” on our side. Any backup you send to Google Drive goes to your own Google account, under Google’s terms, not to us.

Your master password

This is the most important section in these Terms.

When you set up KeyVault, you create a master password. That password is the key that decrypts your vault. It is the foundation of KeyVault’s security model: because encryption happens on your device with a key derived from your master password, your vault data cannot be decrypted without it.

Zetro Labs cannot recover your master password

Your master password is never sent to, stored by, or known to Zetro Labs. There is no password-reset email, no support reset, and no back door. If you forget your master password and you do not have your emergency recovery kit, your vault cannot be opened and its contents will be permanently inaccessible. Neither Zetro Labs nor anyone else can undo this — it is a direct consequence of the encryption that protects you.

You agree that:

  • You are solely responsible for choosing a strong master password, remembering it, and keeping it confidential.
  • You will not share your master password with anyone. Anyone who has it — together with access to your device or a backup of your vault — can open your entire vault.
  • Biometric unlock (such as fingerprint), where you enable it, is a convenience layered on top of your master password. It does not replace it, and you may still be asked for your master password — for example after a restart, after changing security settings, or when Android requires it.
  • If you believe your master password has been compromised, you should change it promptly in the App, update the credentials stored in your vault as needed, and create fresh backups.
  • Loss of access to your vault because you forgot your master password, lost your recovery kit, or lost your device is not a defect in the App and does not entitle you to any remedy from Zetro Labs.

Emergency recovery kit

KeyVault offers an emergency recovery kit — a recovery key shown to you once (as text and as a QR code) that can restore access to your vault if you forget your master password. Using it starts a one-time recovery session in which you must set a new master password.

Treat the kit like the master password itself

Anyone who obtains your recovery kit and a copy of your vault or backup can open your vault. Store the kit somewhere safe, private, and offline where possible — for example, printed and kept in a secure place. Do not store it in the same place as your vault backups, and do not share it with anyone you would not give your master password to.

You agree that:

  • Storing the recovery kit safely is entirely your responsibility. Zetro Labs does not keep a copy of your recovery kit and cannot reissue it.
  • If you lose your recovery kit and forget your master password, your vault cannot be recovered by anyone.
  • If you suspect your recovery kit has been seen or copied by someone else, you should generate a new kit (which invalidates the old one where the App offers that option), change your master password, and create fresh backups.
  • The checksum built into the recovery key helps catch typing mistakes when you enter it manually — it does not make a shared or photographed kit safe.

Passkeys & autofill

On supported devices and Android versions, KeyVault can create and store passkeys and can offer stored credentials through Android’s autofill and credential-provider frameworks.

  • You control each use. Creating a passkey, signing in with one, or filling a credential happens only when you select KeyVault and confirm. Verify the app or website name shown before you confirm — KeyVault cannot protect you if you approve a sign-in on a lookalike or phishing site.
  • Third-party sites decide. Whether a website or app supports passkeys, and how it handles them, is decided by that third party. Information in the App about which sites support passkeys is provided for convenience, may become out of date as sites change, and is not a guarantee that an upgrade will succeed on any particular site.
  • Upgrading a login. When you use “Upgrade to passkey”, the passkey is created on the third-party website in your browser. KeyVault records the result when you return to the App. If you keep the old password as a backup, it remains your responsibility to manage or remove it; if you remove it, make sure the passkey is registered and working first, as the website’s own recovery process would then apply.
  • Removing a passkey in KeyVault does not always remove it at the website. To fully revoke a passkey, you may also need to remove it in the website’s or app’s own security settings.
  • Notes and files on passkeys. You can attach notes and files to a saved passkey just as you can to a password or passcode. They are stored encrypted inside your vault alongside the passkey, and adding them does not change the passkey itself.
  • Compatibility. Passkey and autofill features depend on your Android version, device manufacturer, browser, and the other app or site. Zetro Labs does not guarantee they will work with every service.

Breach checks

KeyVault’s breach-alert feature checks whether your passwords appear in known data breaches using a privacy-preserving technique (k-anonymity): only a short prefix of a one-way hash of a password is sent for the check, never the password itself, so neither the checking service nor Zetro Labs learns your password from the check.

  • Breach results depend on the third-party breach data available at the time of the check. A password reported as “not found” is not guaranteed safe, and a count tells you how often a password has appeared in breaches — by design, the check cannot identify which company or incident a password came from.
  • Breach checks require internet access and only run when the App is in Online mode and you use the feature.
  • If a password is reported as breached, you should change it everywhere you use it as soon as possible. Zetro Labs is not responsible for any breach of a third-party service or for losses arising from a compromised password.
